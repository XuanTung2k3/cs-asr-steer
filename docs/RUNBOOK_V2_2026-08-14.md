## 0. What changed, and why

Two decisions taken on 13–14 August restructure the alignment work.

**Human lexical annotation is dropped.** The claim moves from *accuracy* ("boundaries are within X ms of truth") to *invariance* ("conclusions hold across jitter, conventions, and independent aligner paths"). This is scientifically legitimate, fully automatic, and transfers to any corpus at zero marginal cost. The annotation pack built on 13 August is superseded, not failed; its switch-universe enumeration is reused.

**A third aligner path is added.** Cross-attention pseudo-labels, following the language-alignment method of Liu et al., IEEE TASLP 2025 (arXiv 2403.05887). That paper explicitly rejects CTC for frame-to-token alignment because blank frames carry no language attribute, and reports that cross-attention pseudo-labels outperform forced alignment computed from a comparable-strength model.

> **Outcome, 15 August.** The third path was built, run on the real model, and measured. In the preregistered configuration it does not recover language spans, and it is **not** in scope as a span path. Two span paths remain: CTC under a named blank convention, and Whisper DTW. The negative result is recorded in §4 and in `docs/V2R3_CROSS_ATTENTION_SPANS_2026-08-15.md`; the decision above is retained as the record of what was decided on 13–14 August.

Net effect: the decisive experiment (oracle screen) moves from Day 7 to **Day 4**.

## 1. Gate A, redefined

**Gate A is a development measurement, not a held-out confirmatory gate.**

Exposure ledgers, immutable gate generations, and held-out accounting exist to protect a preregistered claim evaluated once. That is `D-test`. An alignment audit is a preprocessing measurement reported in Experimental Setup.

### What Gate A no longer requires

- Absolute lexical boundary accuracy against gold. The corpus has no gold word timings, human annotation was deliberately dropped, and no absolute-accuracy claim is made.
- A human `manual_lexical` reference.
- Allocation or consumption of a gate generation.
- The 100 ms / 200 ms precision targets, which were inherited from a superseded design that pooled encoder frames over exact spans.
- The 30 ms EN−ZH criterion **as applied to cross-aligner disagreement**. That bound was specified for signed lexical error against gold. Cross-aligner disagreement is a different quantity with much larger natural variance, and is reported descriptively.

### What Gate A now requires

1. A **provisional aligner configuration**, frozen on development evidence, with a recorded config hash.
2. An **invariance battery** (Day 6) showing that the headline conclusion is preserved across boundary jitter, blank conventions, independent aligner paths, and pooling schemes.
3. Boundary statistics **reported by language class** throughout, as a descriptive confound check.

Gate A passes when a provisional configuration is frozen and the invariance battery shows no conclusion flipping. It does not certify accuracy, and the paper does not claim it.

### Aligner paths in scope

**There are two span paths, not three.**

| Path | Status |
|---|---|
| CTC forced alignment under a named blank convention | In scope. Characterised (Day 1); provisional convention `blank_to_preceding`, not frozen |
| Whisper DTW | In scope. Reference for disagreement statistics; 17.4% invalid units on development roles (§13) |
| Cross-attention pseudo-labels (Liu et al., 2025) | **Not in scope.** Built and evaluated on the real model (Day 2, 15 Aug); did not recover language spans in our configuration. Negative result, §4 |

The cross-attention path was implemented and measured, not skipped. It produced
no usable spans in the preregistered configuration and supplies no span
estimates to eligibility, to the Day 3 selection rule, or to the Day 6
invariance battery. Every downstream count of "aligner paths" in this document
means **two**.

## 2. Eligibility rule (current)

An eligible unit is an embedded-English lexical unit with:

- aligned span duration above the tier floor (≥400 ms conservative; ≥300 ms and ≥200 ms as expansion tiers);
- matrix-language preceding context;
- **not utterance-final** (added 14 August — see Day 1 findings);
- non-contiguous reference-unit indices excluded.

There is deliberately **no alignment-confidence condition**. `freeze/l1b_operating_point.json` records `selected_tolerance_ms: null` and `qualifying_tolerances_ms: []`; therefore no valid frozen confidence threshold exists. The high/medium/low labels in the older `consensus_spans_v1.parquet` were produced while no tolerance qualified and are not a valid eligibility filter. Eligibility rests on the duration floor and the structural conditions above. The Day 6 invariance battery carries the robustness argument.

Pooling is a Hann taper over the aligned interval, normalised to sum 1, applied identically to positive and matched control spans. Central-60% and uniform full-span are retained as Day 6 sensitivity variants.

## 2A. Clustering and partition

The independence unit is **`dialogue_id`**, not `conversation_id` or `speaker_id`. CS-Dialogue contains 100 two-party dialogues recorded by 200 speakers, with each side stored separately. Conversation- and speaker-level counts therefore overstate the number of independent sessions by roughly twofold; in this corpus those two existing fields identify one side of a dialogue.

`dialogue_id` is derived by pairing consecutive integer corpus Speaker IDs. The derivation was validated on all 100 pairs: group size was two in 100/100 pairs, topic sets were identical in 100/100 pairs (mean within-pair Jaccard 1.0000; chance pairing produced a mean of 6.84 exact matches and at most 16 across 2,000 draws), and official split membership was shared in 100/100 pairs (chance mean 53.29 and maximum 65). These checks establish the pairing as corpus structure rather than filename-order inference.

Roles are dialogue-atomic. The previous partition had seven pairs of roles sharing dialogues, including eight dialogues shared by `D-dev-select` and `D-dev-confirm`; selection and confirmation consequently shared sessions, topics, and interlocutors. The corrected allocation is:

| Role | Dialogues |
|---|---:|
| `D-construct` | 20 |
| `D-dev-select` | 20 |
| `loc-train` | 15 |
| `util-train` | 12 |
| `D-dev-confirm` | 10 |
| `router-calib` | 8 |
| `D-test` | 15 |

`D-test` remains exactly the official test split and was not reallocated.

The corpus caps the total independent clusters at 100, leaving approximately 8–20 dialogue clusters per role. This is below the roughly 30 clusters commonly recommended for an ordinary cluster bootstrap. Inference therefore uses a wild cluster bootstrap with `t(G-1)` critical values, and every result reports its dialogue-cluster count.

## 2B. Balance and rerandomization

The gated criteria remain `max_categorical_tv <= 0.15` and `max_smd <= 0.25`. They are acceptance constraints on the candidate draw set, following rerandomization (Morgan & Rubin), rather than terms that can be traded off inside the objective. The objective itself is unchanged:

```text
max(max_SMD, max_gated_TV) + w * ungated_TV
```

with the predeclared `w = 0.3`, and it ranks only feasible draws. The earlier implementation ranked all draws by the objective. Once gated TV fell below max |SMD|, further improvement in gated TV stopped affecting the leading term, so the argmin could prefer a gate-failing partition. The acceptance-rule repair clears that defect without changing any threshold or the scoring formula.

The 0.15 categorical-TV threshold was verified attainable by exact enumeration over integer count vectors. Exact floors across every role size were 0.016–0.060 for both gated categorical covariates. The earlier premise that 0.15 was structurally unreachable at dialogue granularity was false, and no threshold amendment was issued.

| Proposal mechanism | Feasible draws | Budget | Feasibility rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Uniform permutation | 1 | 20,000 | 0.005% |
| Stratified deal | 12 | 20,000 | 0.060% |

The accepted partition came from the stratified-deal proposal. Stratification changes only the proposal distribution: it does not change the feasibility constraints or the objective.

The corrected partition improves categorical balance and the objective while satisfying every gated criterion:

| Quantity | Previous | Corrected | Gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Maximum gated categorical TV | 0.2118 | 0.1279 | <=0.15 |
| Maximum |SMD| | 0.2235 | 0.2240 | <=0.25 |
| Objective score | 0.352314 | 0.336514 | descriptive ranking |

The slight max-|SMD| change is reported exactly: 0.2240 is marginally higher than 0.2235, but both are inside the unchanged 0.25 gate. All four earlier gated failures are cleared.

Region balance improved in every role, with maximum ungated TV falling from 0.4294 to 0.3750. The tradeoff appears in topic balance: maximum topic TV rose from 0.1368 to 0.1852; `util-train` and `router-calib` worsened while three roles improved. Region and topic remain ungated, and neither was gated or reweighted. Region remains the largest single imbalance at 0.3750 in `router-calib`.

RNG derivation changed from `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)` to independent deterministic streams per proposal mechanism:

```python
numpy.random.default_rng(
    numpy.random.SeedSequence([seed, proposal_index, 0xC5A5])
)
```

The v2 allocation is therefore **not** a counterfactual reproduction of v1 under a different threshold. The acceptance rule was corrected and the proposal machinery was rebuilt, so v1 and v2 are not comparable draw-for-draw.

## 2C. Artifact publication and supersession

Two publication mechanisms are used deliberately:

- **Role generation:** write the complete generation in an attempt-private directory, write the completion manifest last, then atomically rename-promote it. Publication refuses to start if any destination artifact or sidecar already exists.
- **Candidate generation:** write once at the final generation path behind a completion gate. The destination is refused if it exists at all, so an interrupted run cannot be resumed or reused as a cache. `generation_complete.manifest.json` is written only after every configured family reports `ok` and the table re-verifies. A partial directory **is visible at the final path during a run**.

Both are adequate under the same consumer contract: a generation without its completion manifest must be treated as absent. Attempt-private promotion improves visibility hygiene during role generation, but it does not change what any consumer may validly read.

The namespace supersession chain is **v1 -> v2 -> v2r2**. The `supersede_at` helper enforces `vN -> vN+1` from the freeze filename. Because v2r2 preserves spec version v2 while correcting its recorded partition identity, the same-version v2 -> v2r2 link uses `supersedes_v2_freeze`, which carries the prior path, sealed SHA-256, file SHA-256, and old role-partition fingerprint. If v2r3 is ever required, generalise `supersede_at` rather than adding a third supersession mechanism.

Repository file hashes are size-prefixed. `csasr.utils.hashing.sha256_file` hashes the decimal file size before the file bytes, so plain `hashlib.sha256(file_bytes)` will not reproduce recorded repository hashes. This distinction has caused confusion twice and must be retained in provenance checks.

Artifact identity binds to the full source snapshot, so all code, config, and test edits must be complete before publishing any artifact that a later run will authenticate against. Publishing before finishing test additions caused the v2r2 -> v2r3 republication: six regression tests added after the v2r2 publish changed the snapshot hash and invalidated the identity binding of artifacts published minutes earlier. The supersession chain is now **v1 -> v2 -> v2r2 -> v2r3**.

**Where identity verification applies.** Identity verification applies to artifacts a session publishes, and to artifacts consumed by code that already existed when they were published. It cannot apply to artifacts consumed by newly written code, because identity asks whether the artifact was produced by the code now running — necessarily false in that case. Bytes are the property that matters for reading. This was the fourth occurrence of the pattern and the first where the consuming module itself was the cause: writing `v2r3_oracle_screen.py` changed `code_config_sha256` and thereby invalidated the Day 3 directions it was written to read. Such an input is classified for format, authenticated by byte hash against its sidecar, and recorded as `identity_required: false` with the reason — the standard `lss_v2_candidates._parents` already applies to role manifests. The split gate remains in force for everything a session publishes.

## 3. Day 1 — CTC blank conventions · **COMPLETE (13–14 Aug)**

Four named conventions recomputed over cached candidates, development roles only (`D-construct`, `D-dev-select`), 3,028 paired targets.

### Results

| Convention | Median | P90 | ≤100 ms | ≤200 ms | EN med | ZH med | EN−ZH |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `blank_excluded` | 440 | 1,260 | 8.22% | 23.94% | 240 | 680 | −440 |
| `blank_to_preceding` | **340** | 1,220 | **10.40%** | **30.45%** | 280 | 420 | **−140** |
| `blank_to_following` | 680 | 1,460 | 1.75% | 8.26% | 560 | 720 | −160 |
| `blank_midpoint` | 480 | **1,000** | 3.01% | 12.25% | 400 | 590 | −190 |

### Findings

**The blank-ownership hypothesis is partially confirmed.** Median blank run at language switches on natural speech is 220 ms — not the ~800 ms figure from `GATE_A_CURRENT_STATUS_2026-08-13.md` §5.1, which was a seam-relative measurement on *synthetic spliced* items and therefore a different population. Blank reassignment accounts for roughly 100 ms of a 440 ms median. It is a term, not the term.

**`blank_to_preceding` reduces the class asymmetry 3×**, from EN−ZH −440 ms to −140 ms. This matters more than the median improvement: class-asymmetric boundary error enters the paired contrast `d_i = x⁺ − x⁻` directly and contaminates the direction itself.

**Utterance-final targets are a separate, unaddressable mechanism.** 600 of 3,028 targets (19.8%) are utterance-final, disagree at 700 ms median, and are **unchanged by every convention** — consistent with DTW absorbing trailing silence into the final token, which involves no between-language blank run. Excluding them takes the working median from 440 ms to 280 ms under `blank_to_preceding`.

**Switch universe is clean.** Of 2,428 transitions: 2,347 (96.7%) actionable single-blank-run, 56 zero-length, 25 non-contiguous. No overlaps, no unusable edges, no non-finite boundaries, no third-language interruptions.

### Consequences

- Utterance-final exclusion added to the eligibility rule (§2).
- `blank_to_preceding` is the **provisional** CTC convention, pending three-way comparison. Not frozen. *(Amended 15 Aug: the three-way comparison was run and returned no third path — §4. The convention remains provisional and unfrozen; it is now pending the two-path comparison and the Day 6 battery instead, and no result of the cross-attention run was used to select it.)*
- Residual 280 ms median after best convention and utterance-final exclusion remains unexplained. This is a reason not to over-invest in repairing CTC.

### Reproduction criterion (re-baselined 14 Aug)

The original criterion mixed an all-role paired count (9,728) and P90 with a diagnostic prohibited from reading four of six roles — arithmetically incompatible. Option 1 was chosen: keep development-only scope, re-baseline to that population. Median, EN median, ZH median, and EN−ZH must match documented values exactly; paired count must equal the development-role target count. P90 removed from the criterion. All conditions hold.

## 4. Day 2 — Cross-attention pseudo-labels · **COMPLETE (15 Aug) — negative result**

Full record: `docs/V2R3_CROSS_ATTENTION_SPANS_2026-08-15.md` (Slurm 40711, `COMPLETED`, 142 s).

**What was run, as preregistered.** Frozen Whisper-large-v3, one teacher-forced forward pass on gold transcripts over `D-construct` and `D-dev-select` only. Cross-attention averaged over all 20 heads of the final decoder layer; each encoder frame takes the language of its argmax token; contiguous same-language frames grouped into spans. No DTW, no monotonic path. Decoder layer, head set, smoothing (9 frames = 180 ms), minimum span (5 frames = 100 ms), and special-token handling (own class, never assigned a language) were all fixed in the module source before the first run and recorded in every manifest.

**Result: the configuration does not recover language spans.**

| Diagnostic | Value |
|---|---|
| Disagreement vs the four CTC conventions | 2.77–3.21 s median (**~3 s**), P90 8.9–9.1 s, ≤100 ms agreement ≤0.23% |
| Disagreement vs `whisper_dtw` | 3.16 s median, ≤100 ms agreement 0.67% |
| Frames assigned to the special-token class | **93.2%** (436,155 of 467,991) |
| Cross-attention mass beyond the utterance's own duration | **79.8%** mean, 94.2% max — Whisper applies no encoder attention mask and pads every input to 30 s |
| Spans ≥400 ms in `D-dev-select` | **3**, across 2 dialogues — against **409** across 20 dialogues for CTC |
| EN−ZH signed median difference | −2,480 to −2,996 ms, roughly 35× CTC's −71.7 ms under `blank_to_preceding` (the v2r3 Session 8 figure; the −140 ms in §3 is the older v1 sample of the same quantity) |

For scale: CTC and Whisper DTW disagree with *each other* at a 340–694 ms median depending on convention. This path disagrees with **both** at ~3 s. The three-way agreement the Day 3 selection rule assumed does not exist in this configuration.

The 93.2% figure is **not** a token-mapping bug. The mapping was audited on CPU over 50 utterances: exactly 5 special tokens per utterance (4 prefix + EOT), 21.2% of tokens, with correct spot-checked decodings. 21% of tokens winning 93% of frames is the attention-sink phenomenon, visible on both axes — the token axis (prefix and EOT absorb the argmax) and the frame axis (80% of mass on padding).

**Scientific boundary.** No parameter was tuned to improve these numbers, no path was selected, no floor was set, no gate was evaluated. The 442 surviving spans are retained as evidence of the failure mode and must not be consumed downstream as labels. Every figure above is `cross_aligner_disagreement` between two independent automatic paths on natural speech, never error against truth.

### What this does not establish, and what is deferred

This does not establish that cross-attention pseudo-labels cannot work — only that this configuration does not. Four candidate differences from the cited method were identified and **none was tried**:

1. renormalising attention over **text tokens only**, excluding special tokens before the argmax rather than after;
2. excluding padded encoder frames **before** normalisation rather than after;
3. a different decoder layer, or a head subset selected on an independent criterion;
4. explicit attention-sink correction of the kind now standard for this failure mode.

Each is a **different method**, and each requires its own preregistration and its own run. Adjusting this run's frozen parameters until the numbers improve is the move the repository forbids and was not made. All four are **deferred to after Gate B**; the padding mask (2) is the most likely cause, given that 79.8% of attention mass lands beyond the audio. Until then, Day 3, Day 6, and the paper's alignment paragraph operate on two paths.

**Real-model validation delivered anyway.** The run doubled as the first real-model check and passed on its own terms: empirical 20.0009 ms/frame against an expected 20.0; decoder pad query rows correctly excluded (20,048 rows, essentially all their mass); diagnostic taint on all artifacts; nothing written to production paths; exposure ledger unchanged and no gate generation allocated. The absent encoder attention mask was found by check 2 — a finding, not a defect in the harness.

## 5. Day 3 — Freeze config, build subset, construct directions

**Selection rule, fixed in advance:** prefer the aligner path with highest agreement to the other two independent paths, breaking ties by lower boundary variance within span-duration strata. This is a provisional choice supporting an invariance claim, not a certified-accuracy claim. Record the config hash.

**The rule as written assumed three paths and there are two** (§1, §4). With two paths, agreement is symmetric and cannot discriminate between them, so only the tie-breaker — lower boundary variance within span-duration strata — carries any weight. That is a degenerate selection rule, and treating it as a decisive one would overstate it. Which of the two paths is provisional, or whether both are carried into Day 6 in place of selecting one, is a human decision on development evidence, recorded with its reason. It must not be resolved by whichever path produces the better downstream number.

**Conservative oracle subset** from `D-dev-select` under §2 eligibility at the ≥400 ms tier, baseline-error units only, central-mass pooling. Low coverage is expected and correct.

**Construct directions** on `D-construct`: `Δ^E` at candidate encoder layers; `Δ^{D⊥}` at candidate decoder layers with the prompt subspace projected out of each contrast before aggregation.

Report: `cos(Δ_raw, Δ_residualized)`; ridge energy fraction removed; speaker-bootstrap cosine stability; `corr(‖d_i‖, duration)` and `corr(‖d_i‖, confidence)`; for Site D, `cos(v_nat, U_prompt)` and projection energy removed.

**Numerical requirement:** if shrinkage LDA is run as the estimator ablation, perform covariance estimation and inversion inside an orthonormal basis of the complement of `U_prompt`, then map back.

## 6. Day 4 — Oracle screen · **decisive**

Teacher-forced only. C00 / C10 / C01 / C11 across candidate layers and ρ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}.

**Controls, same batch, not deferred:**

| Control | Answers |
|---|---|
| Wrong location (random position, same utterance) | Is the span meaningful? |
| Matched-norm random direction, ≥5 draws | Direction or perturbation? |
| Opposite sign | Is the axis directional? |
| Label-permuted, ≥5 draws | Does the language pairing carry the effect? |

**Do not implement pair permutation.** For a paired mean difference, permuting which positive pairs with which negative leaves the direction algebraically unchanged: `Σᵢ(h⁺ᵢ − h⁻_π(i))/N = mean(h⁺) − mean(h⁻)`. Permute **labels** within nuisance-matched strata and rebuild through the full pipeline including residualisation. Report `‖Δ_null‖/‖Δ_real‖` per draw.

**Report:** Δ gold-token logprob, gold rank, margin, entropy, with conversation-block bootstrap CIs and **paired** contrasts against each control. Dose–response over ρ per site. Natural-overlap / Mahalanobis displacement for **C11 separately**, since `Δ^D` was estimated on unintervened decoder states.

**Wrong-location does double duty.** If steering the CS span beats steering a random position in the same utterance, the spans localise something real — without any gold reference. This is what replaces annotation, and it is the honest substitute.

## 7. Day 5 — Decision, or offset sweep

**If the effect is present** (correct direction beats all four controls with non-overlapping paired CIs, monotone saturating dose–response): expand eligibility 400 → 300 → 200 ms, measure effect against coverage, then run a first free-decoding check on the conservative subset.

**If absent:** run the offset sweep before concluding. Same direction and strength at −300, −150, 0, +150, +300 ms, reported separately for English and Mandarin spans.

| Result | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Peak at non-zero offset | Systematic span displacement | Correct offset, rerun Day 4. The peak location estimates the displacement — measured without gold. |
| Flat curve | No causal leverage at these sites | Gate B fails. Activate the preregistered fallback. Do not attempt further alignment repair. |

## 8. Day 6 — Invariance battery

Rerun the Day 4 headline result under:

1. Boundary jitter ±50 / ±100 / ±200 ms, **separately for English and Mandarin spans** (by language class, as everywhere in this document)
2. All four CTC blank conventions — `blank_excluded`, `blank_to_preceding`, `blank_to_following`, `blank_midpoint`
3. **Both aligner paths** — CTC under a named blank convention, and `whisper_dtw`
4. Pooling: Hann taper, central-60%, uniform full-span

**The path axis has two levels, not three.** The cross-attention path was built and measured (§4) and does not recover language spans in the preregistered configuration, so it contributes no span estimates to this battery. Including it would not test invariance; it would inject a path that disagrees with both others by ~3 s and place a known-broken estimator inside a robustness claim. The battery is correspondingly narrower, and the paper must say so rather than implying three-path invariance. Whether a corrected cross-attention method is added later is deferred to after Gate B (§4, §13).

`whisper_dtw`'s 17.4% invalid units on development roles must be quantified against two-way agreement with `existing_ctc` before this battery relies on it (§13). With only two levels on the path axis, a weakness in either one is no longer diluted by a third.

**Pass condition:** sign and approximate magnitude preserved in every cell; no conclusion flips.

Also report the EN−ZH signed boundary difference per path, descriptively. This section is the paper's alignment evidence, replacing an accuracy table.

## 9. Day 7 — Gate B

Free decoding on the conservative subset and expansion tiers.

**Pass condition:** net utility positive with conversation-block bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero; corrections exceeding corruptions; correct direction beating every required null in paired bootstrap contrasts.

**Required stratification: deletion vs substitution.** Site D cannot act at a decoder step that does not exist, so a deleted word is structurally beyond its reach; a pooled Site-D result averages a mechanism that cannot fire with one that can.

**Headroom:** eligible units, baseline errors, cluster count, embedded share of error mass, bootstrap MDE — on `D-test` reference-only counts and C00 baselines (permitted; these are not intervention outcomes) as well as `D-dev-select`.

## 10. What the paper says about alignment

Draft into Experimental Setup once Day 6 completes:

> Word-level timing annotations are unavailable for this corpus, and forced alignment on code-switched speech is known to be unreliable (Liu et al., 2025). We therefore make no claim about absolute boundary accuracy. Instead we derive spans from two independent automatic paths — CTC forced alignment under an explicit blank-frame convention, and Whisper DTW — and show that our conclusions are invariant to the choice between them, to boundary jitter of up to ±200 ms in either direction, to all four blank conventions, and to the pooling scheme. Boundary statistics are reported by language class throughout.
>
> We additionally implemented a third path, cross-attention pseudo-labels following Liu et al. (2025), and report it as a negative result. In our configuration — final decoder layer, all heads averaged, per-frame argmax over raw attention — 93.2% of frames were assigned to the special-token class and the resulting spans disagreed with both other paths by a median of approximately 3 s, yielding 3 spans above 400 ms in our development-selection role against 409 from CTC forced alignment. Diagnostics attribute this to attention sinks on both axes: Whisper applies no encoder attention mask, so a mean of 79.8% of cross-attention mass falls on padding beyond the audio, and the prefix and end-of-transcript tokens dominate the token axis. We did not tune this configuration toward the published behaviour, and we exclude the path rather than report a tuned version of it.

Two paths with a reported negative third is a **stronger** methods section than two paths presented without explanation. It states what was tried, what failed, and why, and it forecloses the obvious reviewer question — "why not the method you cite?" — with a measurement instead of silence. The alternative, tuning the configuration until it reproduced the cited paper's behaviour and then reporting only the tuned version, would have been selection on the outcome. Report the failure; do not launder it.

Defensible, requires no annotation, transfers unchanged to any corpus added later.

## 12A. Limitations

- **Cluster count is corpus-limited.** CS-Dialogue contains only 100 independent two-party dialogues. Per-role counts are approximately 8–20, below the roughly 30 clusters usually recommended for an ordinary cluster bootstrap. This is a corpus property, not a remediable implementation defect. Results therefore use a wild cluster bootstrap with `t(G-1)` critical values and always report `G`.
- **Region remains imbalanced.** Maximum region TV is 0.3750 in `router-calib`, even though it improved from 0.4294 and improved in every role. Region plausibly correlates with accent and code-switching habits, which a language-direction intervention could otherwise pick up. This covariate is documented but remains ungated and unweighted pending an explicit scientific decision.
- **Alignment disagreement remains incompletely explained.** Run A leaves a residual 280 ms median CTC–Whisper disagreement after the best blank convention and utterance-final exclusion. Its v1-sample result is stale until Session 8 reconfirms it on v2r2.
- **The invariance claim spans two aligner paths, not three.** The cross-attention path was built, measured, and excluded as a negative result (§4). Invariance across paths is therefore evidence over `existing_ctc` (MMS-FA forced alignment) and `whisper_dtw` only. These remain two genuinely independent estimator families — different acoustic model and different alignment mechanism — so the two-family requirement is not weakened; what narrows is the breadth of the invariance evidence, from three cells on the path axis to two. The paper states the reduced path count and the negative result explicitly (§10); it does not claim three-path invariance.
